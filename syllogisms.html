Syllogisms

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Syllogisms test pure logical validity, not real-world likelihood. A conclusion is valid only if it holds in EVERY possible arrangement consistent with the statements — not just the most obvious one. Drawing every possible Venn configuration (not just the first one you imagine) reveals whether a conclusion truly always follows.

The fast way

Trick

Draw Venn circles for each category. “All A are B” = A fully inside B. “Some A are B” = overlapping circles. “No A are B” = separate circles.

Confusion

“Some A are B” does not imply “Some A are not B.” “All A are B” does not reverse to “All B are A.”

Fix

Ignore real-world plausibility entirely — test only against every possible diagram consistent with the premises.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

All cats are animals. Some animals are dogs. → Some cats are dogs. Valid?

Invalid. There's a valid diagram where the dog-circle never touches the cat-circle, so the conclusion doesn't necessarily follow.

Now you try — answers hidden

All roses are flowers. No flower is a weed. Conclusion: No rose is a weed. Valid?
Valid. Roses are entirely inside flowers, and flowers are entirely separate from weeds — in every possible diagram, roses and weeds can't overlap.
