Section one

Time, Speed & Distance

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

What matters for “closing the gap” between two moving things is their relative speed. Moving toward each other → speeds add. Same direction → subtract.

For a train crossing something, picture the front of the train as a dot. That dot must travel from the start of the object to its end, plus the whole train has to clear it too — so total distance = train length + object length (0 for a pole).

The fast way

Trick

km/h → m/s: ×5/18. m/s → km/h: ×18/5. Boats & streams: still-water speed = average of up/down speeds; current speed = half their difference.

Confusion

Forgetting to add BOTH lengths (train + platform) when crossing a platform, not just the train's own length.

Fix

For every train question, ask “what total distance does the front of the train travel?” before computing.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

A 150m train crosses a 250m platform in 20s. Speed in km/h?

Distance = 400m. Speed = 400/20 = 20 m/s → 20×18/5 = 72 km/h.

Now you try — answers hidden

A boat's speed downstream is 20 km/h and upstream is 12 km/h. Find the boat's speed in still water and the current's speed.
Boat speed = (20+12)/2 = 16 km/h. Current speed = (20−12)/2 = 4 km/h.
Two trains of length 120m and 180m move toward each other at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. Time to cross each other?
Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Total length = 300m. Time = 300/25 = 12 seconds.
