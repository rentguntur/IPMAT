Section one

Time & Work

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Days-to-finish and “work per day” are inverses. If A finishes in 12 days, A completes 1/12 of the job daily — that's A's rate. Rates ADD when people work together; days do not add or average simply. Always convert to rates first.

The LCM trick: instead of “whole job = 1,” say “whole job = 36 units” (LCM of the given day-counts). Now every rate is a whole number — same math, easier arithmetic.

The fast way

Trick

Assume total work = LCM of all given day-counts, turning fractional rates into clean integers.

Confusion

“Twice as efficient” means half the time, not double — efficiency and time move in opposite directions.

Fix

Always convert to rate (work/day) before combining people. Never add raw day-counts.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

A: 12 days, B: 18 days. They work together 4 days, then A leaves. Days left for B?

LCM=36. A's rate=3/day, B's rate=2/day, combined=5/day. In 4 days: 20 units done, 16 remain. B alone: 16/2 = 8 more days.

Now you try — answers hidden

A can do a job in 10 days, B in 15 days. How long will they take together?
LCM=30. A=3/day, B=2/day, combined=5/day. Time = 30/5 = 6 days.
A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish a job in 12 days. How long would A alone take?
If B's rate = r, A's rate = 2r. Combined = 3r = 1/12 → r=1/36. A's rate = 2/36=1/18 → A alone takes 18 days.
