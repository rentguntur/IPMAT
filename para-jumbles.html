Para-jumbles

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

A paragraph has internal logic — sentences reference each other through pronouns and connectors. A sentence starting with “This” is dependent on something mentioned earlier, so it can never open the paragraph. Finding these dependencies (mandatory pairs) narrows the ordering far faster than guessing the sequence from scratch.

The fast way

Trick

Identify the sentence that cannot be the opener, then eliminate every answer option that starts with it.

Confusion

Multiple sentences can look like plausible openers — check whether a later sentence actually depends on it before picking.

Fix

Use the answer options to eliminate — check which are structurally impossible first, rather than building the paragraph blind.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

(1) This led to a decline in local employment. (2) The factory closed in 2010 due to rising costs. (3) Workers had to migrate to nearby cities. (4) Government subsidies failed to revive it. Order?

2-4-1-3. Sentence 1 starts with “This” → can't be the opener. 2 introduces the closure, 4 extends it, 1 states the consequence, 3 follows as the further effect.

Now you try — answers hidden

(1) It was later revived by a group of alumni. (2) The school was founded in 1920. (3) By 1990, it had shut down due to lack of funding. (4) Today, it serves over 500 students. Order?
2-3-1-4. Sentence 1 starts with “It” referring to the school — can't open. 2 introduces the school, 3 gives its decline, 1 gives the revival, 4 gives the present state — a clean chronological chain.
