Vocabulary

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Most English vocabulary is built from a fairly small set of Latin and Greek roots. Learning “bene-”=good and “mal-”=bad doesn't just teach two words — it decodes dozens of unfamiliar words (benevolent, malevolent, malfunction, benefactor). This beats memorizing isolated word-meaning pairs.

The fast way

Trick

For analogies, define the exact relationship in a short phrase before scanning the options — vague matching leads to wrong picks.

Confusion

Words with multiple meanings (e.g. “novel” = new, or a book) — context decides which sense is intended.

Fix

Check the connotation of the original word (positive/negative/neutral) first, and eliminate any option with a mismatched connotation.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

PRODIGAL is to WASTEFUL as FRUGAL is to ______?

Thrifty — the relationship is a synonym pair, applied to the second word.

Now you try — answers hidden

Choose the closest synonym for GARRULOUS: (a) shy (b) talkative (c) angry (d) intelligent
(b) talkative — garrulous means excessively talkative.
BENEVOLENT is to MALEVOLENT as OPTIMISTIC is to ______?
Pessimistic — the relationship is antonym pairs.
