Simple & Compound Interest

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal every time. Compound interest adds each period's interest back into the principal, so the next period's interest is on a slightly bigger amount — that “interest on interest” is what makes CI outgrow SI, and the gap widens over time.

For exactly 2 years, that extra amount equals P×(R/100)² — a shortcut from expanding the CI formula, so you skip computing full CI when only the difference is asked.

The fast way

Trick

“Compounded half-yearly” means double T and halve R before applying the formula.

Confusion

Applying the annual rate directly when compounding isn't annual — a very common trap.

Fix

Rewrite R and T adjusted for compounding frequency before touching the formula.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

Find CI − SI on ₹8000 for 2 years at 5% p.a.

8000 × (5/100)² = 8000 × 0.0025 = ₹20.

Now you try — answers hidden

Find the CI on ₹10,000 for 2 years at 10% p.a., compounded annually.
Year 1: 10,000×1.10=11,000. Year 2: 11,000×1.10=12,100. CI = 12,100−10,000 = ₹2,100.
₹5000 is invested at 8% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year. Find the amount.
Half-yearly: R=4%, T=2 periods. Amount = 5000×1.04² = 5000×1.0816 = ₹5,408.
