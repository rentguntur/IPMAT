Seating Arrangement & Puzzles

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Every clue eliminates some fraction of possible arrangements. A vague clue eliminates few possibilities; a precise clue (“X sits exactly between Y and Z”) pins things down immediately. Processing vague clues first often builds a picture that later gets contradicted, forcing a restart. Start with the most restrictive clue to avoid that backtracking.

The fast way

Trick

Always draw the diagram — never hold the arrangement in your head. Use a consistent notation across all questions in a set.

Confusion

In circular arrangements, “left” and “right” flip depending on whether people face in or out — a huge, common error source.

Fix

If a puzzle isn't resolving in ~90 seconds, test each answer option directly against the clues instead of solving from scratch.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

5 people sit in a row. A is immediately left of B. C is at an end. D is exactly in the middle. Where can E sit?

D=3 (middle). C=1 or 5. If C=1, remaining seats 2,4,5 for A,B,E — the A-B block only fits at 4,5. So A=4, B=5, and E=2.

Now you try — answers hidden

6 people sit around a circular table facing the center. A is second to the right of B. C is immediate left of A. Where is C relative to B?
A is 2 seats clockwise from B (facing center, right = clockwise). C is immediately left of A = 1 seat counter-clockwise from A, which places C one seat clockwise from B.
