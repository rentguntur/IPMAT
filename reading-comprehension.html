Section two

Reading Comprehension

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

RC tests whether you can locate and interpret information quickly, not whether you enjoyed the passage. Reading the whole passage first without knowing what you'll be asked means absorbing detail you don't need and forgetting detail you do. Reading questions first turns passage-reading into a targeted search.

The fast way

Trick

For inference questions, the answer is usually a reworded version of a sentence you can locate — not something you must independently deduce.

Confusion

Options that are factually true but never actually stated in the passage (the out-of-scope trap) are the most common wrong-answer type.

Fix

For every option ask: “can I point to the exact line that supports this?” If not, eliminate it.

Worked examples

Worked example — Mini example

“Although the committee approved the budget unanimously, several members privately expressed reservations about its long-term sustainability.” What can be inferred?

Public agreement didn't fully reflect private opinion. Trap answers: the budget “was rejected” (not stated), or members “opposed it publicly” (contradicts “unanimously”).

Now you try — answers hidden

“Despite the new policy's popularity among employees, several department heads warned it could strain the budget within two years.” What's the safest inference?
Popularity with employees and financial risk are two separate, coexisting facts — the passage doesn't say the policy will be reversed or that employees are wrong, only that a risk was flagged. Correct inference: the policy has both support and unresolved financial concerns.
