Daily Plan & Speed Tactics

The exam, precisely

Indore vs Rohtak — why your strategy should split

IPMAT Indore: 90 questions, 120 minutes, but each section is individually locked at 40 minutes — QA Short Answer (15 Qs, no negative marking at all), QA MCQ (30 Qs, +4/−1), VA MCQ (45 Qs, +4/−1). Total 360 marks (QA MCQ 120 + QA SA 60 + VA 180). Since SA has zero penalty for wrong answers, attempt every single SA question, even on a guess. Verbal Ability has historically been the highest and most stable cutoff section — it decides selection more than any other part.

IPMAT Rohtak: 120 MCQs (40 QA + 40 LR + 40 VA), 120 minutes, no sectional lock, uniform +4/−1 across all three. That's 1 minute per question on average — time management across the whole paper matters more than in Indore, since you can move time between sections freely.

What this means for training: Indore needs deep per-section accuracy (a weak section can't be covered by a strong one). Rohtak needs fast overall triage across a flexible pool of questions. We train both, but prioritize based on which exam matters more to you.

The four phases

  1. Concept mastery — one topic, taught fully, until it's automatic. (Where we are now.)
  2. Topic-wise timed drilling — same concept, now against the clock, in isolation.
  3. Sectional mocks — mixed topics within one section, real time constraints.
  4. Full mocks + error log — the real exam experience, then ruthless review.

Today's routine (Phase 1–2)

20–25 min

One new concept, taught fully — understand it before you speed it up.

20 min

Timed drill on that concept — 15–20 questions, aim under 60 seconds each.

10 min

Revisit yesterday's concept — one quick timed set, no new teaching, pure retention check.

15 min

Verbal, every single day — vocabulary roots plus one RC passage or grammar set. Never skip this, given how decisive VA is.

End of day

Log every mistake: what you got wrong, why, and the fix. This one habit separates toppers from everyone else.

Speed tactics that actually move the needle

Guessing math

In Indore's SA section: attempt everything, zero downside. In MCQs with negative marking: only guess after eliminating at least 2 of 4 options.

Section budgeting

Indore: since sections are locked, rehearse each section's 40-minute budget separately in practice — don't train only on unified 120-minute mocks.

The 90-second rule

If a question crosses 90 seconds with no clear path, mark it and move — one hard question can cost you 2–3 easy ones elsewhere.

Foundation first

Toppers consistently recommend re-solidifying NCERT Maths from classes 6–10 before layering on shortcut techniques — shortcuts without foundation break under pressure.

Error log template

Use this format for every mistake, every day

Topic: — Question: — My answer: — Correct answer:

Why I got it wrong: (concept gap? silly slip? misread the question? ran out of time?)

The fix: (what will I do differently next time I see this pattern?)

Review this log every weekend. Patterns across a week matter more than any single mistake.
