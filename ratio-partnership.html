Ratio, Proportion & Partnership

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

A partnership isn't just about how much money each partner put in — it's about how much “capital-time” each partner contributed. ₹10,000 invested for 12 months has done twice the work of ₹10,000 invested for only 6 months. Profit splits according to (capital × time), not capital alone.

Think of it like renting out money: the longer it's working in the business, the bigger the share it earns.

The fast way

Trick

Convert any ratio straight to “parts of the whole” (2:3:5 → 10 total parts) instead of setting up algebraic equations.

Confusion

Forgetting to multiply by time when partners invest for different durations — comparing raw capital instead.

Fix

Write a quick “Capital × Months” table before computing any partnership ratio, every time.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

A invests ₹5000 for 12 months, B ₹6000 for 8 months. Profit ratio?

A: 5000×12=60,000. B: 6000×8=48,000. Ratio = 5:4.

Now you try — answers hidden

A starts a business with ₹3000. B joins after 4 months with ₹4500. Find the profit ratio at year end.
A: 3000×12=36,000. B: 4500×8=36,000. Ratio = 1:1.
₹720 is divided among A, B, C in the ratio 2:3:4. Find C's share.
Total parts = 9. C's share = 4/9 × 720 = ₹320.
