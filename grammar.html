Grammar & Sentence Correction

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Subject-verb agreement depends only on the true subject — but English often places descriptive phrases between subject and verb, and those phrases can contain a noun that “sounds” like the subject. In “Each of the students have submitted,” the true subject is “Each” (singular); “students” is just part of a prepositional phrase.

The fast way

Trick

Read only for subject and verb, ignoring everything between them — catches most agreement errors instantly.

Confusion

Dangling modifiers are hard to “hear” — a sentence can sound fine read quickly while still being grammatically wrong.

Fix

Physically cross out prepositional phrases before checking subject-verb agreement.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

Find the error: “Each of the students have submitted their assignment.”

“Have” should be “has” — subject is “Each” (singular). Cross out “of the students” to see it clearly.

Now you try — answers hidden

Find the error: “Neither of the answers were correct.”
“Were” should be “was” — “Neither” is singular; “of the answers” is a prepositional phrase to ignore.
Find the error: “The list of items are on the table.”
“Are” should be “is” — subject is “list” (singular), not “items.”
