Section one

Profit, Loss & Discount

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

Three quantities matter: CP (cost price — what the seller paid), MP (marked price — the sticker price before discount), and SP (selling price — what's actually paid).

Two rules almost every mistake in this topic comes from breaking: Profit% or Loss% is always calculated on CP. Discount% is always calculated on MP, never CP.

Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP × 100 SP = MP − Discount

The technique that makes this fast: track the multiplying factor instead of rupees. “Marked up 40%” → ×1.40. “20% discount” → ×0.80 (you keep 80%). If two or more % moves happen on the same base value one after another, multiply the factors together — you never need MP or SP as actual numbers unless the question asks for a rupee amount.

The fast way

Trick

For successive discounts, multiply the retained fractions (not the discounts): 20% then 10% discount → 0.80×0.90=0.72 retained → single equivalent discount is 28%, not 30%.

Confusion

“SP is 20% more than CP” and “CP is 20% less than SP” sound reversible but are not the same statement mathematically — the base is different in each.

Fix

Fix CP = 100 whenever only percentages are given, and derive every other value as an actual number from there.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

A trader marks goods 25% above cost and gives a 10% discount. Find his profit %.

Multiplier: 1.25 × 0.90 = 1.125 → 12.5% profit. No need to compute CP, MP, SP as rupees — the multiplier gives the % directly.

Worked example — Medium — the reverse trap

A shopkeeper sells an article at a 20% loss. Had he sold it for ₹100 more, he'd have made a 5% profit. Find CP.

SP at loss = 0.80×CP. SP at profit = 1.05×CP. Difference = ₹100.
1.05CP − 0.80CP = 100 → 0.25CP = 100 → CP = ₹400.
The moment you see two SP scenarios tied to one CP with a stated rupee difference, set up the subtraction algebraically first — don't compute each SP separately.

Worked example — Exam favorite — successive discounts

Find the single equivalent discount for successive discounts of 20% and 10%.

Multiplier: 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72 retained → discount = 28%. Trap: adding 20+10=30% is wrong — the second discount applies to an already-reduced price.

Worked example — False weight (disguised profit%)

A trader claims to sell at cost price but uses an 800g weight instead of 1kg. Find his profit %.

Profit% = (True − False)/False × 100 = (1000−800)/800 × 100 = 25%. False weight acts like CP (what's actually given), true weight acts like the claimed value (what's charged for).

Now you try — answers hidden

A man buys an item for ₹500 and sells it at a 15% profit. Find the SP.
SP = 500 × 1.15 = ₹575.
A shopkeeper marks goods 50% above cost but allows a 20% discount for cash. Find his profit %.
Multiplier: 1.50 × 0.80 = 1.20 → 20% profit.
Two successive discounts of 10% and 15% are given on a ₹800 marked price, and the trader still makes 8% profit. Find the CP.
SP = 800 × 0.90 × 0.85 = ₹612. Profit 8% means SP = 1.08×CP → CP = 612/1.08 = ₹566.67.
