Section one

Percentages

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

"Percent" means "per hundred." 25% just means 25 out of every 100 — it's a ratio put on a common scale so different things become comparable. "45 out of 60" is hard to compare to "38 out of 50" until you convert both to percentages (75% and 76%) — now they're on the same scale.

x% of y = (x/100) × y

Every percentage question is really this one relationship (part, whole, percentage) viewed from a different angle: find the percentage, find the part, or find the whole. Same equation, different unknown.

Percentage change is different from “percentage of” — it asks how much something grew or shrank relative to where it started:

% change = (New − Old) / Old × 100

The denominator is always the ORIGINAL value — never the new one. This is where marks get lost, because using the wrong base “feels” fine when you're moving fast.

Why “up 50%, down 50%” isn't back to zero: ₹100 up 50% → ₹150. Now ₹150 down 50% → you lose 50% of 150, which is 75, leaving ₹75 — not ₹100. The second move was calculated on a bigger pile than the first, so it doesn't cancel out.

You can get this net effect in one line instead of two steps:

Net % change = a + b + (ab/100)

Check: a=+50, b=−50 → 50 − 50 + (50×−50)/100 = 0 − 25 = −25%. ₹100 at −25% = ₹75. Matches.

The fast way

Trick

Memorize these fraction equivalents cold, don't calculate them: 1/2=50%, 1/3≈33.33%, 1/4=25%, 1/5=20%, 1/6≈16.67%, 1/7≈14.28%, 1/8=12.5%, 1/9≈11.11%, 1/11≈9.09%, 1/12≈8.33%.

Confusion

A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not return you to the original — net is −25%, because the base changed between steps.

Fix

Say “% change = (New − Old)/Old” in your head before plugging in numbers, until it's automatic.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy — build the reflex

A number is increased by 25%, then decreased by 20%. Net % change?

Net = 25 − 20 + (25×−20)/100 = 5 − 5 = 0%. “25% up, 20% down = no change” is a recurring disguised pattern in DI sets — worth memorizing as a shape, not just a formula.

Now you try — answers hidden

A shirt's price is increased by 30%, then decreased by 10%. Net % change?
30 − 10 + (30×−10)/100 = 20 − 3 = 17% net increase.
If A's income is 25% more than B's, by what % is B's income less than A's?
Let B=100, A=125. B is less than A by (125−100)/125×100 = 20%. Note this is NOT 25% — the base flips, so the answer changes.
