Averages, Mixtures & Alligation

Understand it

The concept, explained properly

A simple average treats all items equally. But when you mix unequal quantities of two things, the resulting average leans toward whichever quantity is bigger. Alligation captures this leaning directly from the numbers.

Ratio of quantities = (b − m) : (m − a)

Here a and b are the two prices, m is the mixture average. The ratio pairs the cheaper item's quantity with the gap on the expensive side — a bigger gap on one side means more weight is needed on the corresponding item to pull the average that far.

This only works for a one-time mix. Repeated replacement (diluting a solution again and again) needs an exponential formula instead, because each repetition acts on an already-changed mixture.

The fast way

Trick

Draw the alligation as a literal cross: a and b on the left ends, m in the middle, differences read diagonally.

Confusion

Students often flip which quantity goes with which gap — remember it's always the FAR gap that pairs with a given item.

The repeated-replacement trap

Alligation only works for a one-time mix. If a container is repeatedly drained and refilled (e.g. "20L removed and replaced with water, done 3 times"), alligation does NOT apply — each repetition acts on an already-diluted mixture, not the original. That needs the exponential formula instead:

Final quantity = Initial × (1 − replaced/total)n

Confusing these two is the single most common mistake on this topic.

Fix

Before reaching for alligation, ask: “does this happen once, or does it repeat?” One-time mix → alligation cross. Repeated process → the exponential formula.

Worked examples

Worked example — Easy

Mix ₹40/kg rice with ₹60/kg rice to get ₹52/kg. Ratio?

(60−52):(52−40) = 8:12 = 2:3 (cheaper:dearer). Check: (2×40+3×60)/5 = 52 ✓

Worked example — Hard — repeated replacement

A container has 80L of milk. 20L is removed and replaced with water. Repeated twice more (3 times total). Final milk left?

Fraction retained each time = (80−20)/80 = 3/4. Final milk = 80 × (3/4)3 = 80 × 27/64 = 33.75L. Notice this is NOT 80−60=20 — each removal takes out a mix of milk and water, not pure milk, which is exactly why it compounds instead of subtracting linearly.

Now you try — answers hidden

In what ratio must a 20% acid solution be mixed with a 50% acid solution to get a 30% solution?
(50−30):(30−20) = 20:10 = 2:1 (20% soln : 50% soln).
A vessel has milk worth 25 marks on some scale, another has 40. Mixed to get 30. Ratio of quantities?
(40−30):(30−25) = 10:5 = 2:1.
A tank has 100L of pure milk. 10L is drawn out and replaced with water, done twice more (3 operations total). Final quantity of milk?
Fraction retained each time = (100−10)/100 = 9/10. Final milk = 100 × (9/10)3 = 100 × 729/1000 = 72.9L.
